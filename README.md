# Digitaltwin_hospital
Managing a hospital is incredibly challenging. Patients don't arrive on a predictable schedule, there's never enough ICU beds or diagnostic equipment to go around, and someone who looks stable right now might take a turn for the worse an hour later. Most hospitals still rely on pretty straightforward queue management—either you get seen in the order you arrived, or you're assigned a priority level when you check in. The problem is that it assumes your urgency level stays the same, which obviously isn't how things work in practice. We designed a framework that can actually keep up with these changes as they happen.
What we've built has three core pieces. First is the patient digital twin—a mirror image of the actual patient that updates continuously based on their current state. These twins pull information from bedside monitors, lab systems, medical records, and wearable sensors to stay accurate. Second, we embedded a heuristic engine inside each twin that calculates standard clinical measurements like NEWS2, SOFA, qSOFA, MEWS, and the Rothman Index. All these scores feed into what we call the Dynamic Priority Score (DPS). Third, we created a scheduler inspired by how operating systems work that uses this score to reorganize queues and move resources around.
We implemented a working prototype and tested it with fourteen simulated patient scenarios covering acute, semi-acute, and normal conditions. The system successfully tracked multi-parameter vitals in real-time, computed composite DPS values ranging from 38.0 to 76.2 across the patient cohort, and demonstrated dynamic queue reordering when patient conditions deteriorated. The dashboard visualized resource utilization across ICU beds, HDU beds, ward beds, CT scanners, and physician availability, with the scheduler responding to DPS threshold breaches by triggering admission and escalation events.
