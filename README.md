# Digitaltwin_hospital
Managing a hospital is incredibly challenging. Patients don't arrive on a predictable schedule, there's never enough ICU beds or diagnostic equipment, and someone who looks stable might deteriorate an hour later. Most hospitals still use straightforward queue management—either first-come-first-served or fixed priority categories assigned at admission. This assumes urgency stays constant, which isn't how things work. A patient who seemed okay initially might crash, while another responds well to treatment. We designed a framework that keeps up with these changes.
Our framework has three core pieces. First is the patient digital twin—a continuously updated mirror of each patient pulling data from bedside monitors, lab systems, medical records, and wearable sensors. Second, we embedded a heuristic engine that calculates NEWS2, SOFA, qSOFA, MEWS, and Rothman Index scores, feeding them into a Dynamic Priority Score (DPS). Third, we created an operating-system-inspired scheduler that uses this score to reorganize queues and allocate resources.
We implemented a working prototype and tested it with fourteen simulated patient scenarios covering acute, semi-acute, and normal conditions. Across a 120-tick (2-hour) simulation, the scheduler issued 29 resource allocation decisions, achieving 100% utilisation of HDU beds, ward beds, CT scanner, and on-call physicians. All 14 digital twins maintained live synchronisation throughout with zero staleness events, and 64 clinical events were detected and re-ranked within a single tick. DPS produced a clinically superior ordering compared to NEWS2-alone correctly elevating acute kidney failure (DPS 83.83, SOFA 14) above cardiac (DPS 82.04, SOFA 10) despite the latter's higher NEWS2 score, and ranking post-surgery recovery last (DPS 9.72) with full specificity.
We're building a system that doesn't just make one decision at admission but keeps adjusting throughout each patient's entire hospital stay based on their evolving condition.
