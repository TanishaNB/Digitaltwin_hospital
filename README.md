# Digitaltwin_hospital
Running a hospital isn't straightforward. You've got patients showing up at random times, never enough ICU beds or scanning equipment to go around, and conditions that can flip from stable to critical in an hour. The way most hospitals handle this right now is pretty basic, people either get seen in the order they arrived, or they're given a priority level when they first check in. The trouble with that approach is it treats urgency like it's fixed, when obviously it's not. Someone who looked fine at admission might suddenly crash, while another patient starts improving after getting treatment. What we wanted to do was build something that could actually track these changes as they happen.
We put together a framework with three main parts. The first part is what we're calling a patient digital twin, basically a continuously updated virtual copy of each patient that pulls in data from their bedside monitors, lab results, medical records, and any wearable devices they have on. The second part is a heuristic engine we built into each twin that runs standard clinical calculations, NEWS2, SOFA, qSOFA, MEWS, and the Rothman Index, and combines them into one number we call the Dynamic Priority Score. The third part is a scheduler we designed using concepts from operating systems that takes this score and uses it to reorder queues and shift resources around.
We built a working prototype and ran it through fourteen different patient scenarios, some acute, some semi-acute, some normal cases. Over a 120-tick simulation that represents about two hours of real hospital time, our scheduler made 29 resource allocation calls and hit 100% utilization on HDU beds, ward beds, the CT scanner, and on-call doctors. All 14 digital twins stayed synchronized the whole time without any data lag, and we caught and reordered 64 clinical events within a single tick. The DPS scoring actually outperformed using NEWS2 by itself, for instance, it correctly bumped acute kidney failure (DPS 83.83, SOFA 14) above a cardiac case (DPS 82.04, SOFA 10) even though the cardiac patient had a higher NEWS2, and it appropriately ranked post-surgery recovery last (DPS 9.72).
What we're really trying to do here is move away from making one triage decision when someone arrives and instead keep adjusting priorities based on how patients are actually doing throughout their whole stay.
